An archival study · 3 minute read

Medina in
silver and stone.

Three early photographs and print scans hold the Prophet’s Mosque as it appeared in the first decades of the twentieth century: its minaret, gate, green dome, and open courtyard.

Still / Life picture deskB. Moritz · 1916 LOC portfolioArchive scans

These are archival image scans, not new photographs. All three are from Bernhard Moritz’s 1916 portfolio held by the Library of Congress. The scans are presented as downloaded, without added HDR, color filters, or tonal adjustments. The Library of Congress describes the portfolio items as photomechanical prints: historical printed reproductions of photographs, rather than original camera negatives.

Image sources · Archive records

Trace each photograph.

  1. Bab as-Salam gate, Library of Congress item 2002715727 — B. Moritz, from the 1916 portfolio Bilder aus Palästina, Nord Arabien und dem Sinai. The LOC record states no known restrictions on publication.
  2. Old minaret and green dome, Library of Congress item 2002715728 — B. Moritz, from the 1916 portfolio. The LOC record states no known restrictions on publication.
  3. Southeast courtyard and garden, Library of Congress item 2002715729 — B. Moritz, from the 1916 portfolio. The LOC record states no known restrictions on publication.

These are historical source scans, not newly shot negatives. Images are served locally from the downloaded archive files and are not retouched, colorized, or enhanced for this site. Library records identify the 1916 portfolio items as photomechanical prints.
